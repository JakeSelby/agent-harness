# PRD Quality Review — Agent Harness (prd-agent-harness-2026-09-23)

## Overall verdict
The decision record is the strongest part of this PRD. The thesis is sharp, non-goals are real, counter-metrics are present and the failing cost figure is reported plainly. The addendum carries rejected alternatives for almost every feature. At launch stakes, four things put it at risk: Status lines that say "implemented (0.13)" for a release that has not shipped; no requirement covering how a stranger's own rules get detectors, which is the first-contact path of the lead capability; no requirement at all for upgrade and migration, even though the 1.0 contract depends on them; and roughly a third of the FRs whose consequences test only part of the requirement. The glossary is thorough, but three terms ("catalog", "mode", "posture") each carry more than one meaning, and downstream extraction will trip on them.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Most decisions are stated as decisions and carry their trade-off. FR-18 gives up plugin hooks because "Plugin hooks would merge with user hooks and fire twice". FR-40 keeps the gatherer offline and states the exfiltration reason. FR-39 records that the feature is Claude Code only because "Codex rejects `allow`". FR-48 resolves the authorization tension in one sentence: "Tightening is therefore not authorization". The addendum's §2 "Resolved contradictions" and §3 "Rejected" bullets give a reviewer who wants to push back something concrete to push against. The weak point is §11. Its fourteen open questions are real rather than rhetorical, but none names an owner, a decision date or the FR it blocks. The largest one, whether the cost thesis has a stop condition, is left as a question even though the primary metric has already failed. The PRD contains no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at all.

### Findings
- **[high]** The cost thesis has no decided fallback (§11 Q10, §10 SM-2, §2.1, §9.2). SM-2 is a primary metric and reads "Current: 1.052, failed; no claim is made". The second functional job is "spend less on agent work". v0.15.0 promises "the first published live cost result". Q10 asks whether "the stop condition (publish the instrument findings and stop chasing a saving)" is "a product decision", and nothing answers it. Epics for §4.6 and §4.12 cannot be sized without knowing whether they continue past a second failed result. *Fix:* decide the stop condition, or make it a `[NOTE FOR PM]` with a named deadline. State what §4.6 and the "spend less" job become if SM-2 never passes, and cut the v0.15.0 bullet to "a published live cost result, pass or fail".
- **[medium]** The open questions do not link to the requirements they block (§11). Q12 ("Which hook events does the current Codex client raise?") gates FR-32's Codex coverage and any build on `SubagentStart`, and it says so, but it is not tied to FR-32 or to a milestone. Q14 bears directly on FR-15's `core_switches_acknowledged`. Q2 bears on FR-65, and Q1 duplicates FR-51's "pending decision (#582)". *Fix:* give each question the FRs it blocks, the milestone that needs the answer, and an owner.
- **[medium]** An FR-16 consequence leaves the mechanism undecided (§4.2 FR-16): "`harness init` writes only keys that differ from the defaults, or a mode overlays the base user configuration; #557 records which." A story cannot be accepted against an either/or. *Fix:* keep the behavioural sentence ("After a default `init`, selecting the `superpowers` mode changes `plan-ceremony`") as the consequence, and move the mechanism choice to §11 against #557.
- **[medium]** The context-cap headroom tension goes unstated (§5 NFR-9, §10 SM-3; addendum §3 Guardrails). The addendum records "The line cap is the one that binds: 199 of 200 lines are in use", and the deferral spike (#430) "has not run". The PRD keeps the cap at 200 while v0.14–v0.16 add governance surface. Unless something is deferred, the next always-loaded rule fails lint. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or decision naming either the rules to defer behind hooks or the condition under which the cap is raised, with the reason recorded.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is little furniture. The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: it names the open-loop problem, the detector-per-rule mechanism, the lint refusal and the `harness usage --rules` report. The differentiation claim is earned and dated: "found no other project that binds a detector to each rule … The scan is dated and is re-checked by 2026-12-01". The addendum names the nearest neighbour (Burnd) rather than claiming a vacuum. The NFRs are mostly bounded: Python 3.9, 200 lines, 2 KiB rows, 90-day price-table age, 0.9 precision, 5% and 2% static-bench tolerances, 60-second heal. The eight UJ protagonists are journey carriers, not a persona gallery, and each maps to a feature area. The gaps are the hot-path performance bound and two adjective NFRs.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Hooks have no latency bound (§5). Command grading (FR-35), untrusted-output neutralising (FR-37), spawn routing (FR-30) and the decision log (FR-27) run on every tool call. The only latency figure is FR-49's "it meets its latency bar", which gives no value and covers providers only. For a tool whose cost thesis rests on turns and friction, per-hook overhead is a product-specific NFR. *Fix:* add an NFR with a p95 per-hook bound and a check that measures it.
- **[low]** Two NFRs are adjectives rather than bounds (§5 NFR-6 "atomic where practical", NFR-10 "without corrupting shared state"). *Fix:* name the operations that must be atomic, and name the concurrency test that proves NFR-10 (for example, N parallel syncs or reservations on one machine).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The PRD has a thesis and says which capability leads. §1 frames the field as "open-loop", and §4.4 states "This is the product's lead capability". Counter-metrics exist and each names what it counterbalances (SM-C1 to SM-C5). SM-C2's cached-prefix example is exactly the kind of trap a counter-metric should guard. Three things dilute the arc. First, repository-process requirements sit in the product FR list. Second, the success metrics measure the instrument's speed and the harness's own prefix, not whether measuring rules changes what a developer does. Third, the first functional job, keeping one working style across Claude Code and Codex, has no metric.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Project-governance requirements appear as product FRs (§4.14 FR-9, FR-64, FR-65; §4.11 FR-53, FR-54). "Every SDLC operation must route through its BMad skill" and "a changed requirement amends this PRD" bind this repository's maintainers, not the CLI a user installs. §7's public surface lists none of them. Epic creation will read them as product stories and the architecture spine may scope them as product components. *Fix:* move them to a "Repository process" section, or tag each with a scope line. Keep the IDs.
- **[medium]** No metric measures the thesis outcome (§10). SM-1 measures time to the first report and SM-3 measures the harness's own standing prefix. UJ-3's climax is "Sam deletes rules measured as never firing and watches the standing context shrink", and nothing measures whether that happens. *Fix:* add an SM such as "at least N of the tester cohort change or delete one or more rules within 7 days of their first report", validating FR-20 and FR-21.
- **[medium]** Cross-runtime projection has no success metric (§10, §2.1 first functional job, §1). The Vision's opening capability and UJ-2 have no SM. SM-4 covers lifecycle only. *Fix:* add an SM validating FR-1, FR-2 and FR-3, for example "on every qualified target, one authored stance change reaches both runtimes with zero hand edits and a capability matrix with no unknown entries".
- **[low]** "Loop" means two different things (§1 vs §4.10 FR-50, §9.2). The Vision says in the present tense that "Agent Harness closes that loop" (by measuring). FR-50 "Close the loop" (acting on the measurement) is planned for v0.15.0. A reader cannot tell whether the loop is already closed. *Fix:* rename FR-50 (for example "Evidence-driven stance promotion"), or qualify the Vision sentence.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Many FRs have exemplary consequences. FR-24 ("reported as unpriced, never as $0, and never at a family rate"), FR-48, FR-56 and FR-60 are directly testable. The dimension is still thin, because story creation leans on this section and it has three kinds of gap. The Status lines misstate what is shipped. FR-61 has no consequences. Twenty-two FRs have exactly one consequence, and for several of those the single consequence skips the FR's main clause. Some thresholds are named ("its line cap", "its token cap", "its latency bar") but never given a value.

### Findings
- **[high]** Status lines contradict their own key (§0 "How to read it"; §4.4, §4.8, §4.11). §0 defines `implemented (0.x)` as "shipped in the named release". Release 0.13 is unreleased: the addendum §1 says "0.13 (unreleased)", and `CHANGELOG.md` on `main` tops out at `[0.12.0]`. Even so, FR-19 reads "implemented (0.13; …)", FR-21 "implemented (0.13: `ruleprobe` 0.1.0 on PyPI …)" and FR-41 "implemented (0.13, session-level signal)". FR-13 and FR-22 correctly say "implemented, unreleased" for the same release. FR-12 reads "implemented (0.9)", but its consequence "A minor release marks at least `claude-code-cli-macos` as required" depends on #533, which is planned for v0.12.1. FR-2, FR-22, FR-30, FR-36, FR-41, FR-44 and FR-52 lead with "implemented" and only then say "partial". FR-34, FR-36, FR-44, FR-46 and FR-52 name follow-up work without a milestone, although the key's form is `planned (vX)`. The PRD's own NFR-13 is claims discipline, and epics will read these lines as done/not-done. *Fix:* give every FR one leading effective state from the four values, put `partial` first where it applies, and give every planned remainder a milestone.
- **[high]** Consequences skip the FR's main clause:
  - **FR-30:** "the routing must survive a mid-session registry change" and "sized to the work" are both untested.
  - **FR-36:** the turn-blocking behaviour itself is untested, and "releases after a bounded number of consecutive blocks" has no number (the repository's own instructions say eight).
  - **FR-52:** "record per-case evidence durably" and "no fixes mid-round" are untested.
  - **FR-58:** "attribute cost to each loaded instruction source" is untested; only arm declaration is covered.
  - **FR-44:** "audit" is untested.
  - **FR-57:** "undo exactly what that sync recorded" is tested only for credential files.

  *Fix:* add one consequence per operative clause.
- **[medium]** FR-61 has no testable consequences (§4.13 FR-61 "Workspaces"), and "shared session store" is used nowhere else and is not in the glossary. *Fix:* add consequences (for example, that sessions from any root in a workspace appear in one store and remain resumable), and define the term.
- **[medium]** Thresholds are named but have no value. FR-43 has "its line cap". FR-38 and NFR-9 have "its token cap"; the value 4,202 appears only in SM-3. FR-49 has "its latency bar". FR-37 uses "instruction-shaped" and "neutralised" without defining either. *Fix:* put each number in the FR that enforces it, and define the FR-37 pattern class or point to its detector list.
- **[medium]** FR-50's promotion mechanism is undefined, and its consequence is an outcome, not a behaviour (§4.10 FR-50). "Let evidence promote or demote a stance variant" does not say whether promotion is automatic or a proposal the developer applies. Automatic promotion would sit against §1 "The developer owns every setting". "At least one variant is promoted or demoted from ledger evidence" depends on data, not on the system. *Fix:* state that promotion is a proposal needing developer approval, or state the bounds on automatic change. Make the consequence behavioural, for example "given ledger rows past threshold X, the report proposes variant Y".
- **[medium]** Some success metrics and one FR lack pass bars (§10, §4.11 FR-12):
  - SM-1 gives a time but no share of the cohort and no cohort size.
  - SM-2's task set is named three ways: "the release task set", "the eight-task set" and, in §11 Q10, "the clean long-task set".
  - SM-6 does not define the window for "retained".
  - FR-12's "until one scripted round has been compared against a hand-driven round" names no agreement criterion.

  *Fix:* give each one a numeric bar and name the task set once.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Omissions are mostly explicit. §8 does real work ("It never caps spend", "The harness is not an MCP server"), and so do §9.3, the per-FR "Out of scope" lines (FR-2, FR-16, FR-63) and honest partials such as FR-34's "zero spawns in 19 headless runs". Two capability-sized holes are unacknowledged, and the assumption mechanism promised in §0 is not carried out. Open-item density is 14 questions, 6 assumptions and 0 notes. That is acceptable for a re-baseline where most FRs are implemented, but the open items cluster on the planned v0.14–v0.15 FRs that epics will build next.

### Findings
- **[high]** Upgrade and migration have no FR (§4.3, §7, §9.1, §10 SM-4). §9.1's lifecycle proof requires "upgrade from the prior baseline", and SM-4 requires every supported target to pass "install, upgrade, repeat sync, rollback and uninstall". §7 says that from 1.0 the migration policy "binds every change to the public surface", which includes the configuration schema, ledger schema and primitive formats. No FR says what upgrade does to `config.json`, the ownership journal or the ledger, nor what "rollback" means across harness versions. FR-17 "Install and update channels" covers channels only. *Fix:* add an FR for upgrade (schema migration, journal carry-over, the meaning of downgrade and rollback) with consequences, and link SM-4 to it.
- **[high]** The first-contact path of the lead capability is unspecified (§2.3 UJ-3, §4.4 FR-19 and FR-21, §4.1 FR-13, §10 SM-1). UJ-3 has Sam run `ruleprobe` against a 300-line file of his own and "see the hit rate per rule". SM-1 promises a first-time developer learns "which of their rules fired" within a minute. However, FR-21 reports hits only "for declarative detectors", and FR-19's registry holds "6 generic" detectors. No FR says how an arbitrary user rule gets bound to a detector (matching, authoring or generation), or what coverage a stranger should expect. FR-13 imports existing rules, which will have neither a detector nor a reason, and FR-19 says lint "must fail" on such a rule. The PRD does not say whether lint applies to external primitive roots. *Fix:* add an FR for binding detectors to user rules and state the expected coverage. State lint's behaviour for imported primitives. Make SM-1 report the share of a stranger's rules that get measured, not only the time taken.
- **[medium]** The 1.0 Codex commitment runs ahead of its evidence path (§9.1, §4.11 FR-12 and FR-52, §11 Q12). §9.1 lists Codex CLI on macOS and Ubuntu as supported targets. Against that, FR-12 keeps Codex targets non-required until a scripted round is compared, FR-52 automates "1 of 11 cases (#336)" with no milestone, Q12 says Codex hook events are unsettled, and the addendum records that 0.11.1 was "the last release qualified on both CLIs". No milestone in §9.2 carries #336 or the Codex comparison. *Fix:* put them on a milestone, or mark Codex 1.0 support as conditional with a `[NOTE FOR PM]`.
- **[medium]** The assumption tags promised in §0 are absent inline (§0 "Every inferred point is tagged `[ASSUMPTION]`"; §12). All six assumptions exist only in the index; FR-25, FR-32, FR-45, §9.2, SM-1 and SM-6 carry no inline tag. The SM-1 index entry also adds a binding definition ("measured from invocation to first report on a directory of up to 30 days of transcripts") that SM-1 itself lacks. *Fix:* tag each point inline and move the SM-1 definition into SM-1.

## Downstream usability — adequate
Extraction will mostly work. The glossary covers about forty terms. FR IDs are unique and cover FR-1 to FR-65 with no gaps. Every feature description except §4.9, §4.10 and §4.12 carries "Realizes UJ-n". Evidence directories are linked, and the addendum's §4 binds each research artifact to the FRs and SMs it supports. The damage comes from term collisions: the same word names different artifacts, and a story author extracting one FR will not see which is meant.

### Findings
- **[medium]** "Catalog" names two artifacts (§3 Primitive and Target; §4.1 FR-1; §4.11 FR-12; §2.3 UJ-7). One is the provider-neutral primitive catalog (FR-1, `harness catalog`). The other is the compatibility catalog ("the compatibility catalog tracks", "the tag, catalog, changelog or About disagree", "a waiver published in the catalog"). Neither has its own glossary entry. *Fix:* add "Primitive catalog" and "Compatibility catalog" entries and use them verbatim.
- **[medium]** "Mode" carries three meanings (§3 Decision provider and Selection model; FR-16, FR-39, FR-43, FR-48). It names a decision point's `shadow`/`advise`/`act` setting ("Each decision point must carry its own mode"), the selection Modes and `HARNESS_MODE`, and the runtime's plan mode. *Fix:* rename the decision-point sense to "stage", which FR-49 already uses ("moves through its stages").
- **[medium]** "Posture" in FR-39 is not the glossary's posture (§4.7 FR-39). "Selected permission posture" and "Under a `bypass` or `auto` posture" use a term the glossary defines only as Cost posture. `bypass` and `auto` are not variants of the `autonomy` stance (`ask`, `confirm-writes`, `execute`). *Fix:* define the runtime "permission mode" and state how it relates to the `autonomy` variants.
- **[low]** Several defined terms have synonyms. See Mechanical notes. *Fix:* replace each synonym with the glossary term.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape matches the product: a brownfield developer CLI with a roadmap. The capability spec is organised by feature, and per-FR Status lines separate what exists from what is planned, which is the brownfield distinction the rubric asks for. The UJs are light and each maps to a feature area, which is proportionate for a mostly single-operator tool. A spot check of existing-code references on `main` (247c7c3) resolved: `primitives/constraints.json`, `adapters/claude-code/capabilities.json`, `adapters/codex/capabilities.json`, `scripts/release_preflight.py`, `scripts/advance_stable.py`, `scripts/sync_about.py`, `scripts/cost_bench.py`, `docs/compatibility-policy.md`, `product.json`, and the `catalog`, `generate`, `import`, `stances`, `tiers`, `integration`, `decisions`, `remote-control`, `worktree` and `usage` subcommands. `harness selection` and `benchmarks/ablations.json` are absent, which is consistent with their planned status. FR-19's count of 17 detectors matches a repository test that names seventeen.

### Findings
- **[low]** Some UJs are missing context lines (§2.3). UJ-5 (Casey) and UJ-7 (Jordan) have no "Persona and context" line, and only UJ-1 has an entry state. *Fix:* add one line of context to each.
- **[low]** Decision providers have no journey (§4.10). FR-47 to FR-50 carry a whole milestone (v0.15.0) but have no UJ and only SM-7. *Fix:* add a short UJ, or state that this feature is capability-only.

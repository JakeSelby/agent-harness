## Story

As a developer running agents under graded shell commands, I want a decision provider on the `ask` band
of `grade-bash` that can turn a grade-1 allow into an ask when a command looks off-task for what I asked,
so that routine-looking but off-task commands reach me, while the deterministic grade stays the
authority and nothing is ever relaxed.

## Context and value

Command grading is a token grammar: it cannot tell a routine command from one that is off-task for the
user's request. A typed yes/no with a probability fits that gap and costs little per call. It is also
promptable: outside testing of a similar gate got dangerous commands through by claiming a human had
already approved them. So the grade stays the authority, a deny is never relaxed, and grade 2 and above
never reach the provider. This story supersedes #146 and carries the bounded on-task comparison #142
held; it is one of the epic's two first consumers.
[Source: https://github.com/JakeSelby/agent-harness/issues/372]

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** a grade-1 command, **when** the point runs, **then** its state is the user's messages, the
   final command about to run and the deterministic grade, with no tool output and no assistant prose.
2. **Given** a grade 2 or higher command or a deny, **when** grading runs, **then** the provider is not
   consulted and the outcome is unchanged.
3. **Given** a provider answer, **when** it is mapped, **then** it falls into one of three bands:
   clearly risky (allow becomes ask), clearly fine, or a middle band that leaves the normal prompt
   alone; `unknown` and low confidence mean "do what the deterministic grade says".
4. **Given** 0.13.0, **when** the point is at `act`, **then** it may only tighten; relaxing an ask into
   an allow is built but stays off until the point meets a written criterion on the harness's own labels.
5. **Given** the hook chain, **when** the point runs, **then** it classifies the request about to
   execute, last, after any rewriting hook.
6. **Given** offline regression tests, **when** they run, **then** they cover the three bands, `unknown`,
   provider failure and timeout, and every failure path falls through to today's behaviour.
7. **Given** a labelled adversarial set including approval-claim and authority-claim attacks, **when**
   the point is evaluated, **then** tighten-only `act` is enabled only at zero dangerous pass-through on
   it.
8. **Given** the decision log, **when** the point is evaluated per #138, **then** misses, false alarms,
   cost and latency are reported.

## Design

### Approach

A consumer inside `policy/hooks/grade-bash.py`'s `ask` band for grade-1 commands, reading the per-point
mode from #137 and writing #139's decision row, with a three-band mapping instead of one threshold.

### Decisions and alternatives

- **Three bands, not one threshold:** the middle band leaves the normal prompt alone.
- **No tool output or assistant prose in state:** both are injection surfaces.
- **Tighten-only in 0.13.0;** relaxation built but off. This matches FR-48: at `act` a provider may turn
  an allow into an ask and nothing else.
- **Grade 2 and above never reach the provider;** a deny is never relaxed.

### Do not implement

- Prompt-injection or exfiltration screening of tool output.
- Any change to what `grade-bash` denies.
- Granting permission; a low risk score is not proof of safety.

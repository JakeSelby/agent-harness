# AH-B086 — The 100 KB grade-bash timing test fails under Python 3.9 on a loaded Mac

## Reproduction

On an Apple-silicon Mac under the system Python 3.9.6, with a load average of 5 to 7:

`cd tests && /usr/bin/python3 -m unittest test_grade_bash.GradeTests.test_grading_a_hundred_kilobyte_command_stays_well_inside_the_hook_timeout`

Two of three runs failed with `AssertionError: 0.259599209 not less than 0.2` and
`0.22669545800000002 not less than 0.2`; the third passed. The same test passes under Python
3.14.5 on the same machine and in CI.

## Root cause

The test timed one run of `grade_text` on a 100 KB command and compared it with a fixed 0.2
seconds. The grader is linear, about 2 microseconds a byte under 3.9.6 and under 1 under 3.14.5:
single runs at 25, 100, 200 and 400 KB measured 0.06, 0.21, 0.38 and 0.64 seconds under 3.9.6. So
0.2 seconds was the floor interpreter's ordinary cost, not a margin, and one sample under load
lands either side of it. The bound was also not tied to anything: its comment said the hook has
five seconds, while `registration` in `lib/harness_core/lifecycle.py` registers every PreToolUse
hook with a 10-second timeout. The grader itself is not slow on 3.9.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** the PreToolUse timeout `harness sync` registers, **when** the test bounds the 100 KB
   grade, **then** the bound is derived from that timeout, not a literal.
2. **Given** a loaded machine, **when** the test runs under the Python 3.9 floor, **then** it
   passes, because it compares the best of several runs.
3. **Given** a grader whose cost grows quadratically enough to dominate at 100 KB, **when** the
   test runs, **then** it fails, whether or not the absolute bound still holds.
4. **Given** Python 3.14, **when** `test_grade_bash` runs, **then** it passes as before.

## Design

`pre_tool_use_timeout()` reads the tightest PreToolUse timeout `lifecycle.registration` produces
for either runtime; the grader may spend a tenth of it, since every PreToolUse policy, interpreter
start and the governance call share that one timeout. Each measurement is the minimum of five
runs, which measures the grader rather than the scheduler. A second assertion compares the 100 KB
grade with a quarter-size one: linear grading measured ratios of 2.9 to 5.8 under load, a
quadratic one approaches 16, and the test fails at 8. Rejected: a looser literal, which neither
names what it protects nor catches a quadratic that fits inside it; deleting the test; skipping it
on 3.9. The grader is unchanged.

## Regression test

`GradeTests.test_grading_a_hundred_kilobyte_command_stays_well_inside_the_hook_timeout` in
`tests/test_grade_bash.py`.
- **Before the fix:** failed two of three runs under 3.9.6 at load 5 to 7.
- **After the fix:** passes under 3.9.6 and 3.14.5 at load 7 to 9. With a quadratic
  `text[i:].lower()` every fifth byte injected ahead of `grade_text`, it fails at growth ratios
  of 10.3 and 8.8.
